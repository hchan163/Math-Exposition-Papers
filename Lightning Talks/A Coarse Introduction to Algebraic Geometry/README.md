Talk: ***A coarse introduction to algebraic geometry***


Abstract: Algebraic geometry is one of the areas of mathematics that is the most difficult to penetrate for outsiders. More than that, for beginners starting in the area it is very hard to see beyond the abstraction and see the geometry that underlies all the algebraic machinery. In this talk we give a motivated introduction to the main idea(s) of algebraic geometry: what it is, why things are the way they are, what does it do.

This is a lighthearted talk meant to illustrate the guiding principles of the area in an elementary fashion. Hence, those more well versed in the language of algebraic geometry should not expect this talk to cover any serious topics (e.g. commutative algebra, varieties, schemes); although I may be convinced to run a course in (classical) algebraic geometry should there be enough interest.

The talk will take approximately one hour, and some familiarity with rings and topology is assumed.

Prerequisites: 
- Ring theory (Rings, Ideals, Homomorphisms, Quotient rings, Polynomial rings)
- Pointset topology (Topological spaces)
